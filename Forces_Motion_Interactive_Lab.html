Beyond the Device: Exploring Forces & Motion

Interactive investigation | Secondary physical science | Approx. 25–35 minutes

1. Your mission

Learning objective: Predict and explain how net force and friction influence an object's motion, collect evidence from a virtual investigation, and apply your findings to a real-world design decision.

Imagine your school needs to move a heavy equipment cart safely across the floor. What forces affect the cart? How can we use evidence to make the task easier?

Accessibility: All instructions and questions are available as text. Use Tab to move between controls. You may work with a partner, use keyboard input, and use the written observations if the external simulation is not accessible on your device.

2. Predict before you test

If two equal forces push an object in opposite directions, what is the net force?

3. Explore a virtual lab

Open the free PhET Forces and Motion: Basics simulation (opens in a new tab). Explore the Net Force and Friction screens. Predict what will happen, change one variable at a time, and observe the results.

  1. On Net Force, compare balanced and unbalanced pulls.
  2. On Friction, compare the same applied force with different friction settings.
  3. Record what happened and explain why.

The PhET simulation is an external educational resource created by the University of Colorado Boulder; the investigation, prompts, and checks on this page are original.

Alternative evidence if the simulation is unavailable: When equal forces act in opposite directions, net force is zero. When one direction has a greater force, net force is nonzero. Friction opposes relative motion and can reduce the effect of an applied force.

4. Check your understanding

5. Apply it to a real problem

Your team needs to move a heavy equipment cart across a classroom. Recommend one practical change that could make moving the cart easier or safer. Use the terms force and friction, and cite evidence from your investigation.

Your responses remain on your device unless you download and share them. No student data is sent to this page's author.

For teachers: reuse and adaptation

Use this investigation for direct instruction, stations, intervention, or independent learning. Adapt the final scenario to carts, sports, bicycles, or engineering design. The downloadable notes can serve as a formative assessment. Verify device compatibility and provide alternative access when needed.
